The boy worked the morning shift at a convenience store. The girl came in for breakfast. This had gone on for several months, and they still did not know each other's names. She smiled when she thanked him. He sometimes meant to say something more, but there was usually someone waiting behind her. On mornings when there wasn't, he said, "Have a good day," just the same.

By then he liked her. He had no idea what she made of him. Perhaps she thought he was a pleasant shop assistant. Perhaps she had never had occasion to think about him at all.

One day, on his way home from work, he came across an advertisement for a pill that allowed you to hear other people's thoughts. He wrote down the address, found the shop, and told the shopkeeper about the girl.

The shopkeeper laid the tablets on the counter.

"Keep one in your mouth and look into the other person's eyes. Don't think about anything yourself. Give them your full attention."

"And I'll hear what they're thinking?"

"Yes. But you must concentrate."

The boy bought two.

The next morning, when the girl entered the convenience store, he put a tablet in his mouth. There were no other customers. She chose her breakfast, brought it to the counter, and looked up to find him watching her.

He did as the shopkeeper had instructed. He let his thoughts fall away and looked into her eyes. She looked back. She didn't ask what she owed. For a while, neither of them spoke.

He heard nothing.

At last he lowered his eyes and told her the total. He put her breakfast in a bag. She paid and thanked him as she always did. He kept his eyes down until she had left, then looked toward the door.

After his shift, he took the unused tablet back to the shop.

"It didn't work," he said. "I couldn't hear anything."

"Were you thinking about something else?"

"No. I wasn't thinking about anything. I just kept looking at her."

"Ah, I see," said the shopkeeper. "If the other person isn't thinking about anything either, if all their attention is on you, you won't hear anything. Even if you can read minds, there has to be something to read."

The boy said nothing. He had come back to make sense of the morning. He was more puzzled now than when he had arrived.

The shopkeeper glanced out the window.

"Is that the girl?"

She was coming down the street with a small paper bag in her hand. At the door she stopped. She had seen the boy inside.

"She bought some this morning too," said the shopkeeper. "I expect she thinks they don't work and wants a refund."
